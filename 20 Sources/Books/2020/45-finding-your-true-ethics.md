# Finding Your True Ethics — Marc Hansen

## Overview

Marc Hansen explores the gap between what we say we believe and how we actually behave. The core insight: most people have an ethical theory (whether they know it or not), and that theory drives behavior more than stated principles. The book is about developing an authentic ethical framework through honest self-examination — not following external rules, but building an internal compass.

## Core Concepts

### The Gap Between Belief and Behavior
Most people don't behave according to their stated ethics — they behave according to their actual (unexamined) ethical theory. The gap between "what I say" and "what I do" is where ethical failures live. The first step is making your actual ethical theory explicit.

### The Three Ethical Theories
The book identifies three basic ethical theories:
- **Consequentialism**: right and wrong are determined by outcomes. If it produces good outcomes, it's right.
- **Deontology**: right and wrong are determined by duties and rules. If it follows the rule, it's right.
- **Virtue Ethics**: right and wrong are determined by character. If it expresses good character, it's right.

Most people blend these — the question is which one dominates.

### The Problem with Rules
Following rules (deontology) is the most common ethical approach — but it's also the most fragile. Rules have edge cases. When the rule conflicts with the outcome, or when the situation doesn't fit neatly into any rule, the person without an internal compass is lost.

### Authenticity
True ethics must be authentic — they must be yours, not borrowed. Following someone else's ethical framework (even a great philosopher's) without examining whether it fits you creates a hollow authenticity. The work of ethics is personal, not academic.

### The Role of Temptation
Ethical failures usually occur at moments of weakness — when we're tired, stressed, or tempted. Building ethical muscle requires practicing ethical behavior in small moments, not just grand gestures. The daily decisions are where character is built.

### The Importance of Accountability
We are not good at evaluating our own ethics objectively. Accountability to others — a trusted advisor, a peer group, a mentor — provides the external perspective needed to see our blind spots.

## Key Lessons

1. Your actual ethical theory drives behavior — make it explicit.
2. Rules are fragile — they fail in edge cases when you have no internal compass.
3. Authenticity requires personal examination — borrowed ethics are hollow.
4. Daily decisions build character — ethics is practiced in small moments, not just grand gestures.
5. Temptation reveals ethical weakness — build muscle through small daily practices.
6. Accountability to others reveals blind spots — we are not objective about ourselves.
7. The gap between belief and behavior is where ethical failures live.
8. True ethics is internal — it cannot be outsourced to a rulebook.

## Practical Applications

- Identify your dominant ethical theory: consequentialist, deontological, or virtue-based.
- Examine your actual behavior: does it match what you say you believe?
- Find an accountability partner: someone who will give you honest feedback on ethical decisions.
- Practice ethics in small moments: how you treat the waiter, the email you don't need to send.
- When facing an ethical decision, name it: "This is an ethical decision, not just a business one."
- Build a personal ethical framework through reflection, not just reading.
- When tempted, pause: the moment of temptation is the moment to practice ethics.

## Controversy/Criticism

The book is less rigorous academically than philosophical classics. Critics argue that "finding your own ethics" can justify any behavior. Others find the blend of self-help and philosophy productive.

## One-Line Takeaway

> True ethics comes from understanding what you really believe, not following rules — make your ethical theory explicit, practice ethics in daily moments, and build an internal compass through accountability.
