# Indistractable — Nir Eyal

## Overview

Nir Eyal (Hooked) turns his attention from creating habits to defeating them. The core argument: distraction is not a technology problem — it's a psychological one. We blame phones and apps, but the real problem is internal: we're trying to escape uncomfortable emotions. The solution is not more willpower or screen-time restrictions — it's understanding and addressing the psychological triggers that drive us to distraction.

## Core Concepts

### The Traction/Distraction Axis
Everything you do either moves you toward your values (traction) or away from them (distraction). Both are behaviors — the difference is intention. Distraction is not about the phone; it's about why you reach for the phone. The external trigger (notification) is not the cause — it's the permission.

### The Four Internal Triggers
Four uncomfortable emotions drive distraction: (1) Loneliness — reaching for connection; (2) Uncertainty — seeking relief from doubt; (3) Exhaustion — seeking rest from mental fatigue; (4) Lack of self-worth — seeking competence. The trigger precedes the urge. Know your triggers.

### Timeboxing
Timeboxing: schedule when you will do tasks, including leisure. This is different from time blocking — timeboxing specifies the time, not just the duration. "I'll work from 9-11 AM" is a timebox. Timeboxing gives structure to both work and play, preventing the blur between them.

### The Distraction Reset
When you get distracted, don't punish yourself — use it as data. The distraction reveals an unmet need. Ask: "What am I trying to escape? What emotion am I avoiding?" The answer points to the real problem.

### Hacking Back
The 4-step method to defeat internal triggers: (1) Remove the external trigger; (2) Remove the behavior; (3) Remove the anticipation; (4) Remove the connection. For example: turn off phone notifications (remove external trigger) → put phone in another room (remove behavior) → use app blockers (remove anticipation) → change the association (remove connection).

### Pre-Commitment
Make doing the right thing easier and doing the wrong thing harder. Lock yourself out of distraction: Cold Turkey, Freedom, or plain physical distance. Pre-commitment is not weakness — it's strategy. The most disciplined people use the most pre-commitment devices.

## Key Lessons

1. Distraction is an internal problem — it's not the phone, it's your attempt to escape uncomfortable emotions.
2. Know your triggers — the four internal triggers (loneliness, uncertainty, exhaustion, lack of self-worth) precede every distraction.
3. Use timeboxing — schedule when you work AND when you play.
4. Distraction is data — when you get distracted, use it as information about an unmet need.
5. Hack back systematically — remove external trigger, behavior, anticipation, and connection.
6. Pre-commitment is strategy — lock yourself out of distraction before you're tempted.
7. Scheduled leakage is fine — planned distraction (checking social media at lunch) is not the problem.
8. Master internal triggers first — external controls are supplements, not substitutes.

## Practical Applications

- Track your triggers: for one week, log every time you reach for your phone and what emotion preceded it.
- Timebox your entire day — work blocks and leisure blocks with specific times.
- Use pre-commitment devices: app blockers, phone in another room, internet schedules.
- Practice the distraction reset: when distracted, ask "what am I escaping?"
- Schedule your leakage — plan when you'll check social media, email, news.
- Distinguish between traction and distraction: is this moving me toward my values?

## Controversy/Criticism

Some find the "internal triggers" framework too psychological — critics argue it doesn't account for genuinely addictive product design. Others find the advice common-sense and not actionable enough. The book can feel like it's blaming users for technology's manipulation.

## One-Line Takeaway

> Distraction is an internal problem projected outward — understand the psychological triggers that drive you to escape, and you can defeat any external distraction.
