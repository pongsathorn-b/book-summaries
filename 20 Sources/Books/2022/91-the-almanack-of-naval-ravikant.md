# The Almanack of Naval Ravikant — Naval Ravikant

## Overview

Eric Jorgensen compiled *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* from Naval's tweets, podcast appearances, and essays. Naval is the founder of AngelList and one of Silicon Valley's most respected investors, but this book is more philosophy than finance. His core message: wealth is a skill, not a circumstance. Specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage (labor, capital, code) can build wealth. Happiness is also a skill — the ability to accept reality, cultivate presence, and generate your own peace. The combination of these skills is what creates a life worth living.

## Core Concepts

### Wealth = Specific Knowledge + Accountability + Leverage
Naval's wealth formula: **(1) Specific Knowledge** — Knowledge that cannot be trained or automated, derived from genuine curiosity and lifelong learning. **(2) Accountability** — Taking personal responsibility for outcomes. **(3) Leverage** — One of three types: labor (people working for you), capital (money working for you), or code/media (products with zero marginal cost). Specific knowledge cannot be taught in school — it can only be learned through passion, curiosity, and years of experience in a specific domain.

### The Four Types of Leverage
**(1) Labor** — People working for you. The oldest form of leverage, but difficult to scale. **(2) Capital** — Money as a multiplier. Requires credibility and track record to access at scale. **(3) Code** — Software and media that scale without proportional cost. The most powerful form of leverage in the modern era. **(4) Media** — Content (podcasts, books, YouTube) with near-zero marginal cost. The democratization of leverage.

### Happiness Is a Skill
Naval's most counterintuitive claim: happiness is not a circumstance — it is a skill. You can cultivate happiness the same way you cultivate strength. The foundation of happiness is **acceptance** — not resignation but acknowledgment that what is, is. The struggle against reality is the source of most suffering. Naval's prescription: learn to be comfortable with being wrong, cultivate equanimity, practice desirelessness.

### The Present Moment and the Peace Model
Naval draws heavily on Eastern philosophy, particularly Stoicism and Buddhism. The present moment is the only place where peace exists — the past is memory, the future is projection. The skill of happiness is the ability to be present without the mind's constant commentary. This is not passivity — it's the foundation from which all effective action happens.

### Seeking Is the Opposite of Happiness
The more you seek happiness, the less you find it. Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want. The solution is not to eliminate desire but to choose your desires carefully. Want what you want, not what society tells you to want. The goal is not to eliminate desire but to select it deliberately.

### Building Judgment
Naval's meta-skill is **judgment** — the ability to make good decisions in the face of uncertainty. Judgment requires specific knowledge, experience, and a clear mental model of how the world works. The best way to build judgment is to read widely, think independently, and maintain a willingness to be wrong and update your views.

### The Power of Reading
Naval reads for hours daily — not for information but for understanding. The goal is not to collect facts but to build mental models. The person who understands the most about a specific domain — and can connect that understanding across domains — has the greatest edge.

## Key Lessons

1. Wealth is a skill — specific knowledge + accountability + leverage. It can be learned.
2. Specific knowledge cannot be taught — it can only be discovered through genuine passion and curiosity.
3. Code and media are the most powerful forms of leverage — they scale without proportional cost.
4. Happiness is a skill — it can be cultivated through acceptance, presence, and desirelessness.
5. The present moment is the only place where peace exists.
6. The struggle against reality is the source of most suffering.
7. Judgment is the meta-skill — the ability to make good decisions under uncertainty.
8. Read for understanding, not information — connect models across domains.
9. Desire is a contract you make to be unhappy — choose your desires carefully.
10. Long-term thinking and patience are compounding mechanisms — most people underestimate them.

## Practical Applications

- Develop specific knowledge in a domain you are genuinely curious about — not what sounds prestigious or lucrative
- Build accountability by taking personal responsibility for all outcomes in your domain
- Learn to code or create media — the most accessible forms of zero-marginal-cost leverage
- Practice daily presence — meditation, or simply sitting with uncomfortable thoughts without reacting
- Apply the "Naval Method" to reading — ask what mental model each book reveals, not what facts it contains
- Choose your desires deliberately — before pursuing something, ask if you would want it if you couldn't tell anyone about it

## Controversy/Criticism

Critics argue Naval's advice to "follow your curiosity" is a privilege available only to those without immediate financial pressure. His specific knowledge framework may underestimate the role of structural barriers and luck. The happiness advice, while valuable, may oversimplify clinical depression and anxiety disorders. Some argue Naval's success was heavily dependent on specific historical conditions (Silicon Valley in the 1990s and 2000s) that are not reproducible.

## One-Line Takeaway

> Wealth is a skill built through specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage — and happiness is a parallel skill built through acceptance, presence, and the deliberate selection of your desires.
