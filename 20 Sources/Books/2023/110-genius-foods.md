# Genius Foods — Max Lugavere

## Overview

*Genius Foods* by Max Lugavere is a practical guide to brain-optimizing nutrition. After his mother's diagnosis with a rare form of dementia, Lugavere — a journalist and self-taught nutrition researcher — embarked on a quest to understand how diet affects the brain. The result is a book that synthesizes neuroscience and nutrition research into actionable dietary guidance for cognitive performance, memory, focus, and the prevention of neurodegenerative disease.

## Core Concepts

### Brain-Optimizing Foods

**Extra-virgin olive oil (EVOO)** — Rich in polyphenols and monounsaturated fats that protect neuronal membranes, reduce inflammation, and support BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor). Lugavere recommends 3-4 tablespoons daily, raw or low-heat cooking.

**Wild-caught fatty fish** — Salmon, sardines, and mackerel are rich in DHA (docosahexaenoic acid), the most abundant omega-3 fatty acid in the brain. DHA comprises 40% of neuronal membranes and is critical for synaptic function, memory, and mood regulation.

**Avocados** — The most nutrient-dense fruit, supporting blood flow to the brain through vasodilation and providing folate, vitamin K, and healthy fats.

**Blueberries** — High in anthocyanins that cross the blood-brain barrier and accumulate in the hippocampus, the brain region critical for memory. Blueberries improve neuronal signaling and reduce inflammation.

**Dark chocolate (85%+ cacao)** — Rich in flavonoids, caffeine, and theobromine that enhance focus, mood, and cerebral blood flow. The ideal dose is 1-2 squares of 85%+ dark chocolate.

**Eggs** — One of the most bioavailable sources of choline, a nutrient critical for acetylcholine synthesis (a neurotransmitter essential for memory and attention).

**Leafy greens** — Spinach, kale, and Swiss chard are rich in folate, vitamin K, and nitrates that support cognitive function and blood flow.

### The Brain-Boosting Dietary Pattern
Lugavere's dietary framework: **(1) Prioritize whole foods** — organic when possible, especially for the dirty dozen. **(2) Eliminate processed foods** — particularly those with added sugars and industrial seed oils. **(3) Include brain-healthy fats** — EVOO, avocados, fatty fish, eggs, nuts. **(4) Eat the rainbow** — a variety of colorful plants provides diverse phytonutrients. **(5) Intermittent fasting** — 14-16 hour overnight fast enhances BDNF and autophagy.

### Blood Sugar and the Brain
The brain consumes 20% of the body's glucose but has no capacity to store it — it depends on a steady supply from the bloodstream. Blood sugar volatility — from high-glycemic foods — damages neuronal membranes and accelerates cognitive decline. Lugavere recommends: **(1)** Eliminate refined carbohydrates. **(2)** Pair carbohydrates with protein and fat. **(3)** Use cinnamon and chromium to stabilize blood sugar.

### The Role of Sleep and Exercise
Lugavere emphasizes that food is only one pillar of brain health. Sleep and exercise are equally critical: **(1) Sleep** — During deep sleep, the brain clears metabolic waste through the glymphatic system; chronic sleep deprivation accelerates amyloid plaque accumulation. **(2) Exercise** — Exercise increases BDNF, promotes neurogenesis in the hippocampus, and improves cerebral blood flow.

### Key Supplements
While food is primary, Lugavere identifies supplements that can fill gaps: **(1) Omega-3 DHA** — if not eating fatty fish 2-3x per week. **(2) Vitamin D3** — most people are deficient, and D3 supports numerous brain functions. **(3) Magnesium threonate** — the only form of magnesium that crosses the blood-brain barrier; supports synaptic plasticity. **(4) Probiotics** — the gut-brain axis is real; gut health affects mood and cognition.

## Key Lessons

1. The brain is uniquely sensitive to what you eat — it requires specific nutrients that most Western diets lack.
2. Extra-virgin olive oil, wild-caught fish, avocados, eggs, blueberries, and leafy greens are the six genius foods.
3. Blood sugar volatility is one of the most damaging things for the brain — control it through diet.
4. Choline from eggs is essential for memory and attention — most people are deficient.
5. Sleep is when the brain clears metabolic waste — chronic sleep deprivation accelerates cognitive decline.
6. Exercise is a nootropic — it increases BDNF and promotes neurogenesis.
7. The gut-brain axis is real — probiotic-rich foods support cognitive function and mood.
8. Intermittent fasting enhances BDNF and autophagy — 14-16 hour overnight fasts are beneficial.
9. Omega-3 DHA from fish is the most important brain-building nutrient — most Western diets are deficient.
10. Prevention is easier than reversal — start protecting your brain in your 20s and 30s.

## Practical Applications

- Eat 3-4 tablespoons of extra-virgin olive oil daily — use it as your primary cooking fat
- Eat wild-caught fatty fish 2-3 times per week — salmon, sardines, or mackerel
- Include eggs daily — the choline is critical for memory and attention
- Add blueberries to your morning routine — 1 cup daily provides cognitive benefits
- Eliminate added sugar and refined carbohydrates — stabilize blood sugar through pairing with protein and fat
- Implement 14-16 hour overnight fasts — stop eating after dinner, eat at midday the next day
- Take omega-3 DHA, vitamin D3, and magnesium threonate supplements if dietary intake is insufficient
- Prioritize sleep — 7-9 hours nightly, treat sleep as non-negotiable

## Controversy/Criticism

Critics argue that some of Lugavere's recommendations are based on preliminary or observational research that may not hold up in clinical trials. The supplement recommendations have been challenged by researchers who argue that whole foods are superior to isolated nutrients. The book's emphasis on individual foods (genius foods) over dietary patterns may oversimplify the complex science of nutrition.

## One-Line Takeaway

> Genius Foods reveals that specific foods — extra-virgin olive oil, wild-caught fish, avocados, eggs, blueberries, and leafy greens — are the most powerful nootropics available, and that eating to protect your brain is both simple and well-supported by science.
